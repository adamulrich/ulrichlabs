Operating Goals

Goals, Problems, Solutions

The go kit is intended to support voice and FM-based digital modes, including Packet/Winlink, APRS, fldigi, and FM FT8.

Problem 1

  • The DigiRig requires a connection to the external speaker port.
  • The audio port is difficult to reach once the radio is installed in the 2U hardcase.
  • The RT-95 disables its internal speaker as soon as something is plugged into the external speaker port.

Solution

  • An A/B RJ45 switch, A/B audio switch, and external speaker wired correctly support Winlink and voice communications. Together, the audio switch and external speaker provide a practical way to move between radio audio and DigiRig audio.

Problem 2

  • You cannot lock down a faceplate design until all of the components are positioned. Cutting a piece of wood or sheet metal makes iteration challenging.

Solution

  • Use AI to generate 3D-printable parametric SCAD files and iterate on the faceplate design.

Problem 3

  • Mounting the speaker to the faceplate while protecting the wiring during disassembly is difficult. The speaker connection points are tiny, and the small wires are fragile.

Solution

  • Add a 3.5mm jack to the speaker and use a standard audio cable for the connection to the A/B switch.

Problem 4

  • The AC power connection requires a 2-inch hole. The 2U case is 3.5 inches high, but the shelf sits at 1.75 inches.

Solution

  • Cut a section from the shelf to provide the outlet clearance needed.

Problem 5

  • The oval holes in the shelf are not wide enough for some of the wires to pass through.

Solution

  • Drill a hole in the shelf by widening one of the oval openings.

Problem 6

  • The center faceplate section may come loose because it is not screwed to the rails.

Solution

  • Add holes to the four corners of the plate so you can drill small pilot holes into the case plastic and secure it.

Build

Assembly and field setup

The following notes cover the physical modifications, wiring, and assembly sequence for the radio hardcase.

Shelf Mods

  1. Drill a hole in the left side of the shelf for a wire passthrough. I widened an existing hole five rows back from the front.
  2. Using an angle grinder, cut out the section of the shelf in front of the left two holes. This provides clearance for the AC power socket.

3D-Printed Parts

  1. Print all the parts.
  2. In the two A/B switch mounts, use a soldering iron to insert an M3x4x5 threaded brass insert into each standoff. The inserts should be flush with the top of the standoff.

Top of the Shelf

  1. Mount the power supply as shown using screws and nuts.
  2. Mount the radio bracket to the shelf using screws and nuts.
  3. Mount the DigiRig holder using screws and nuts.

Bottom of the Shelf

  1. Mount the RJ45 A/B switch underneath the radio bracket as shown, using the M3 screws and printed washers.
  2. Mount the audio A/B switch underneath the radio bracket as shown, using the M3 screws and printed washers.

Power Wiring

  • Create a common ground using the black wire for the power supply output and the Powerpole input.
  • The power switch selects which hot (red) power source to use.
  • Flipping the switch to the left makes contact with the right terminal. I oriented the switch left and right so the switch points toward the AC or DC input. Wire the DC input on the left to the right terminal, and wire the power supply output hot wire to the left terminal.
  • Wire the center terminal on the switch to the fuse, which then connects to the radio.

Faceplate

  • Mount the speaker to the center faceplate using M3 screws and nuts.
  • I chose to wire a 3.5mm audio jack to the speaker terminals using straight pins from breadboards. The jack could be skipped and wired directly to the speaker, but I am hoping the jack will make maintenance more durable.

The remaining wire routing is left to the builder to arrange as appropriate. The build photographs provide routing examples.

A/B Switches

The microphone output and speaker output on the back of the radio connect to the I/O ports on the switches. I used a 90-degree right-angle RJ45 6-inch cable (in the parts list) and routed it just below the shelf.

  • Wire RJ45 A to the mic jack on the center faceplate.
  • Wire RJ45 B to the DigiRig cable.
  • Wire audio A to the speaker on the faceplate.
  • Wire audio B to the DigiRig cable.

Final Assembly

  • Mount the radio using the thumbscrews.
  • Attach the port cables to the center and right panels.
  • Insert the spacer behind the panel. Check the orientation so the curve matches the case, the holes line up, and the cutout aligns with the shelf. Then screw the right panel in place.
  • Insert the center panel. Note that the panels use a tongue-and-groove fit. Ensure that you have removed the 3D supports under the tongue and in the groove.
  • Insert the left panel, again using the spacer and aligning the tongue and groove, then screw it into place.
  • Drill pilot holes into the center-section corner holes, then gently hand-tighten screws into those holes. The plastic is thin, and it is easy to strip the screws.

The custom enclosure and mounting parts are available as a single 3MF project file for reference or further modification.

Battery Box Wiring

Power and switch wiring

The faceplate was used as the drilling guide for the holes in the front of the radio hardcase.

  • The front-panel on/off switch is not required to be on in order to use the battery. The two paths are wired in parallel using 2x1 wire connectors.
  • The front-panel wiring guide covers the main connections. For the switch wiring, the gold lead is ground, the center lead is the hot wire to the battery, and the top lead goes to the daisy chain of ports.
  • There is an extra black lead that is not used; cut it off.
  • Powerpole connectors were installed to make battery swaps and maintenance easier.
